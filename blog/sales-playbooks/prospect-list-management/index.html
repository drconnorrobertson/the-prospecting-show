How to Manage a Prospect List

Give each account one owner and one dated next action. Keep contacts, touch history, and opt-outs attached to the account so the team can see what happened before anyone follows up.

Set up the list before adding contacts

  1. Create one record per account and use a normalized company domain to flag possible duplicates. Review parent companies and subsidiaries before merging records.
  2. Record the reason the account fits, the evidence source, and the date it was checked. Use an explicit research status when evidence is missing.
  3. Attach contacts and their roles to the account. Keep do-not-contact preferences visible and preserve them when importing or merging records.
  4. Set the next action and due date, or state why a paused account stays on the list and when it should be reviewed.

Keep the list current

Filter the active list for duplicate domains, overdue actions, and missing owners. Resolve those first, then review whether the fit evidence is still current. Report how many active accounts have an owner and a dated next action. Inspect a sample of completed actions to see whether the notes explain the next decision.

Adapt the list to what you sell

  • Leadership training prospect lists for sponsors, cohorts, and reviewers.
  • Professional coaching prospect lists for program fit and follow-up.
