Sales training agenda: scoping and practice templates

The Prospecting Show · Editorial sales resource · Published October 12, 2026 · Updated November 22, 2026

A sales training agenda needs a clear purpose, timed activities, an observable skill, and an owner for follow-through. Use the 30-minute agenda below when a buyer and training provider are deciding what a program should cover. Use the 60-minute agenda when a facilitator is running a practice session with reps.

Both are editable planning templates. The role-play and timing allocations are illustrative, not a tested promise of higher conversion, revenue, or rep performance. Adapt the scope, group size, accessibility, and pace to the participants. The example focuses on asking a factual discovery question and accurately restating the answer.

Choose the decision and bring the right inputs

For a buyer scoping meeting, the decision is whether a defined training need and a workable delivery plan exist. Invite the sales or enablement owner who can describe the current workflow and approve a next step. Bring one approved, anonymized example of the behavior being discussed, or use a fictional scenario if real examples are unavailable.

For a rep practice session, the decision is which behavior each learner will practice next and how the manager will review it. Prepare a shared role-play, a short observation rubric, and a way to capture feedback. Neither meeting needs private customer records, an unsupported savings estimate, or a long generic capabilities presentation.

The durations reconcile: 5 + 7 + 8 + 6 + 4 = 30 minutes. If the buyer cannot identify a relevant behavior or available participants, record that finding. Do not convert an exploratory conversation into an approved training program merely because the calendar slot ended.

A 60-minute sales practice agenda

The example uses pairs practicing in parallel. Explain the roles and observation rules before starting. For a larger group, choose an appropriate facilitation plan and enough time for each participant; do not assume every rep can complete a long individual presentation inside the same hour.

Output: practice notes and an owned follow-through action
MinutesActivityEvidence to capture
0–5 · 5 minutesFacilitator states the single skill, explains the fictional scenario, and confirms participation needs.Shared objective and roles.
5–12 · 7 minutesFacilitator demonstrates the question and restatement, then discusses what the buyer actually said.One example linked to the observation rubric.
12–22 · 10 minutesPairs run a first attempt: four minutes per learner and two minutes for brief notes.Observed behavior and words, without inferred motives.
22–30 · 8 minutesPairs discuss one useful behavior and one specific revision; facilitator resolves questions.A defined change to try next.
30–42 · 12 minutesPairs repeat the task: five minutes per learner and two minutes for notes.A second observation using the same criteria.
42–52 · 10 minutesGroup compares what changed, what stayed unclear, and which assumptions were unsupported.Practice findings and unresolved questions.
52–60 · 8 minutesLearners choose a next practice action; manager confirms the review owner, evidence, and follow-up arrangement.An owned action and a review plan.

The durations reconcile: 5 + 7 + 10 + 8 + 12 + 10 + 8 = 60 minutes. Time allocations are planning choices. If a discussion needs longer, agree what will move rather than silently removing the second practice or the follow-through decision.

A fictional discovery role-play

Buyer brief: a sales leader says reps use different evidence to move an opportunity into a qualified stage. Some rely on a meeting being booked; others wait for a scoped buyer problem. The buyer has not established the effect on conversion or revenue and has not approved a training purchase.

Learner task: ask how the qualification decision happens today, listen, and restate the answer without adding an unverified consequence. Then identify what evidence would help the buyer decide whether training is relevant.

Question: “What evidence does a rep currently need before moving an opportunity into the qualified stage?”

Fictional buyer response: “There isn't a common rule. Each rep uses their own judgment.”

Possible restatement: “The stage decision currently depends on each rep's judgment. Have I understood that correctly? What would your team need to agree before a shared rule would be useful?”

This is one possible exchange. A buyer who says a shared rule already exists has changed the evidence. Respond to that answer instead of continuing a script that assumes a problem. Do not supply an invented cost, emotional motive, or urgent deadline to make the role-play sound persuasive.

Review observable behavior

  • Question: did the learner ask about the actual workflow without supplying the desired answer?
  • Restatement: did the learner preserve the meaning of the response and invite correction?
  • Next evidence: did the learner identify a relevant unanswered question before proposing a solution?

For each criterion, record observed, not yet demonstrated, or not observed in this attempt, with a brief example. Keep “not observed” distinct from failure when the role-play did not provide an opportunity to demonstrate the behavior. Use the same criteria for both attempts and let the learner respond to the feedback.

A stronger second practice attempt is a practice observation. It does not prove that a program caused higher sales performance. Any later evaluation needs its own population, baseline, definitions, review period, and evidence. Attendance and a completed worksheet alone cannot establish a business result.

Download editable agendas

Download the 30-minute buyer scoping agenda (CSV) or download the 60-minute practice agenda (CSV). Each file contains start minute, end minute, duration, owner, activity, and output. They are templates, with no customer or learner information.

Before using either agenda, confirm who owns the meeting, how participants will prepare, which material may be shared, and what happens afterward. A rep practice session should end with an actionable review arrangement; a buyer scoping meeting should end with a decision about fit and evidence.
