Manage a prospect list: fields, review rules and worked example

The Prospecting Show · Editorial workflow guide · Updated November 22, 2026

Manage a prospect list as a set of decisions: which account fits the offer, which role owns the relevant work, what evidence is current, and whether there is a useful next action. A spreadsheet can support a small review and a CRM can coordinate a larger team, but neither fixes unclear definitions or unowned follow-up.

Use one current working record per account and relevant conversation, preserve its source history, and keep duplicate, suppressed, and unresolved records out of the ready queue. The worked example below is fictional. It describes list operations, not a response-rate benchmark or permission to contact a particular recipient.

Use fields that support a decision

  • Stable account key: an internal identifier tied to the actual business entity. A similar trading name alone is not enough to merge two companies.
  • Offer and fit reason: the specific service being evaluated and the operating condition that could make it useful.
  • Relevant role: who may own the work, with confirmed roles distinguished from title-based hypotheses.
  • Evidence reference and verification date: what supports the entry and when it was checked. Preserve previous references when the record changes.
  • Record owner: one person responsible for resolving the next question and coordinating contact.
  • Contact or suppression status: the current stop condition, preference, or unresolved permission issue under the team's approved process.
  • Next action and review condition: what should happen next, who owns it, and the evidence or agreed date that triggers it.

A field marked unknown can be useful. A fabricated buyer, budget, or deadline makes the list less reliable. Keep confidential buyer records outside a prospecting export and reference the approved internal location when needed. The practice downloads contain fictional business records and no real contact details.

Define states before assigning activity

StateMeaningNext action
Duplicate recordAnother record represents the same confirmed account and conversation.Resolve identity, preserve source and suppression history, then link the duplicate to the retained record. Do not add another contact task.
SuppressedAn applicable request to stop or another approved stop condition exists.Keep the suppression effective across imports and campaign copies. Do not automatically restart contact when a source refreshes.
ResearchA required fit, role, freshness, or contact condition is unresolved.Assign one question and an owner. A recent activity date does not establish current evidence.
Ready for next-action reviewThe defined evidence gates are resolved and no recorded stop condition blocks the proposed step.The owner checks the actual channel, recipient relevance, preferences, and agreed process before acting. The label is not blanket permission.
Closed or buyer-requested revisitThe offer is unsuitable, the buyer declines, or the buyer agrees to a specific revisit condition.Record the reason. A revisit date must not override a stop request or create an invented opportunity.

Worked example: six rows do not mean six prospects

Download the six fictional source records (CSV) or download a blank list-review template (CSV). The example uses one proposed conversation per account; a real account may have several legitimate buying roles, which must be coordinated rather than indiscriminately merged.

The import reconciles as 6 raw rows − 1 duplicate = 5 unique accounts. Those five accounts reconcile as 1 suppressed + 2 research + 2 ready = 5. The ready share of unique accounts is 2/5, or 40%; the ready share of raw imported rows is 2/6, about 33.3%. State which denominator you use. Neither figure measures delivered messages, replies, meetings, or sales.

If R02 had contained a stop request, retaining R01 would not erase it. Resolve the suppression history before assigning any ready state. Likewise, a different employee at the same company is not automatically a duplicate contact; distinguish account identity from person and buying-role identity.

Review exceptions before expanding the list

  1. Save the import identifier and source references so the reviewed population can be reconstructed.
  2. Resolve identity collisions and relevant stop conditions before assigning tasks.
  3. Review research records for one actionable missing fact. Leave the record unresolved if evidence is unavailable.
  4. Check ready records for a named owner and a next action supported by current evidence.
  5. Record the result of each action accurately: useful conversation, wrong role, declined, unknown, or another defined outcome.
  6. Reconcile the states back to the unique reviewed accounts. Preserve historical snapshots when comparing periods.

Choose freshness checks according to what can change. A new job title may require role verification; a changed service requirement may invalidate fit; a stop request needs prompt handling through the approved process. There is no universal refresh interval that proves every record is usable. Define your rule and record the reason for exceptions.

Measure list quality separately from campaign performance

Track unresolved-role share, stale-evidence share, duplicate findings, suppression handling, and records with an owned next action. When measuring a campaign, use its actual eligible population and delivery or conversation records. Do not turn the list's ready count into a claimed reply rate.

A team can improve list quality by closing unsuitable records, even when the ready queue gets smaller. Review recurring mismatch reasons with the person responsible for the offer and targeting criteria. Adding more rows before resolving those mismatches only expands the same uncertainty.
