Map the buying team for professional coaching

The Prospecting Show with Dr. Connor Robertson · Editorial sales resource · October 12, 2026

Find the people who experience, evaluate, approve, and implement a change. This guide applies the workflow to selling professional coaching, where a useful conversation may involve the business owner and team leader and a practical review of important decisions repeatedly returning to the owner.

The finished result is a buying-role map. Work from information the buyer confirms. The examples below are planning models; replace bracketed fields and unsupported assumptions before using them with an actual account.

Start with the buyer’s operating context

For this selling context, a possible reason to investigate involves a leadership transition. That event alone does not establish demand. Check whether the team experiences this issue: important decisions repeatedly returning to the owner. Confirm who owns the workflow and whether a change is worth evaluating.

A practical sequence

  1. Identify the likely operational owner.
  2. Distinguish a user from a budget approver and technical reviewer.
  3. Record which roles are confirmed and which remain hypotheses.
  4. Ask the first contact who else must participate in evaluating the issue.

Apply these steps to the same account rather than changing the hypothesis halfway through the exercise. If new evidence contradicts your starting assumption, update the record and reconsider the next action. The objective is a useful decision, not a completed form.

Worked example: map the buying team

Illustrative example, not a report of a client result.

Begin with the business owner and team leader. Record who experiences important decisions repeatedly returning to the owner, who reviews coaching scope, confidentiality expectations, and participation, and who can authorize a change. Ask: “Who would need to confirm that this approach works before you could make a decision?”

Use a concrete artifact

A useful supporting resource in this context is a decision-ownership worksheet. It should help the business owner and team leader assess the current situation without forcing them through a generic sales presentation. Keep the scope narrow enough that the buyer can correct it.

For example, connect the artifact to decision ownership, meeting actions, and follow-through. Identify which inputs came from the buyer, which are public observations, and which remain assumptions. Do not request private operational records before the buyer has agreed an appropriate purpose and handling process.

Complete this worksheet during preparation, then revise it after the conversation. An unknown is a useful finding when it is recorded honestly.

Evaluate the result

Track opportunities with confirmed decision and implementation roles. Define the numerator, denominator, and review window before comparing results. Use comparable accounts and channels; otherwise an apparent improvement may simply reflect a different list or buying context.

For professional coaching, pair the workflow measure with the quality of evidence about important decisions repeatedly returning to the owner. A reply or meeting is useful only if it helps establish relevance. Record declined and unsuitable opportunities so the team can improve targeting as well as messaging.

A common failure to avoid

Collecting many names is not the same as understanding a decision. Do not treat job titles as confirmed authority.

In this context, the practical consequence is spending time on a fit conversation about one recurring leadership decision before the buyer has confirmed a reason to do it. Revisit coaching scope, confidentiality expectations, and participation early enough to discover a genuine mismatch.

When to move forward

Proceed when the contact can describe how a decision would be made and who should join. A sensible next step may be a fit conversation about one recurring leadership decision, but only when it follows from the evidence. If the buyer cannot identify a relevant problem or owner, close or reclassify the record instead of manufacturing urgency.
