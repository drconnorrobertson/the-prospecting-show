Prepare Discovery Questions Around What Is Known and Unresolved

By The Prospecting Show Editorial · August 12, 2026 · Conversation preparation · Sales Call Frameworks

A discovery conversation can leave several different kinds of information: something a participant stated, a number that still needs a source, a seller's interpretation, and a proposed next step. Keeping those categories separate makes the notes easier to review. A friendly conversation alone does not establish a qualified opportunity or a likely sale.

Agree on the conversation's purpose

Begin by asking what the participants want to cover and how much time is available. A proposed agenda is a starting point for agreement, not evidence that the other person has accepted a sales process. If a participant wants a different discussion, record that difference instead of treating the original agenda as confirmed.

Useful opening prompts include: What prompted this conversation? Which question would make this time useful for you? What should we leave for another discussion? These are original preparation prompts, not a tested script or a required sequence.

Ask about the work as the participant describes it

Invite a description of the current process: What happens first? Who handles the next part? Which exception would someone reading a summary otherwise miss? Keep a participant's account identified as their account. It is different from a process observation or an inspected operating record.

When someone describes a difficulty, ask what they mean by it and whether they have an example they can share. Do not assume that the first answer is rehearsed, false or incomplete. A follow-up can request clarification without claiming to know an undisclosed underlying problem.

Leave unknown quantities unknown

If time, cost or frequency matters to the discussion, ask how the figure was obtained, which period it covers and whether it is an estimate. A participant may not have that information. Record the gap rather than building a persuasive number from an unsupported assumption.

For a later comparison, preserve the unit, population and source. Hours spent on one task are not automatically hours that could be removed from the whole workflow. A proposed saving remains a proposal until there is evidence appropriate to that claim.

Explore prior attempts without supplying a failure story

Possible questions include: What has already been tried? What changed afterward? Which parts were observed and which remain uncertain? The answer need not establish that an attempt failed, why it failed or what a future objection will be. Keep those interpretations separate from what the participant actually reports.

If another person or document may hold useful context, ask which source would help and whether there is an appropriate way to obtain it. A mention of another person does not establish that person's authority, participation or agreement.

Describe a next step at its actual status

Ask what, if anything, the participant would find useful next. A request for information, a proposed meeting and a confirmed meeting are different states. Record who would do what and whether the person has agreed. An unanswered suggestion should remain unanswered in the notes.

A recap can separate participant statements, open questions and proposed actions. Review the recipient, scope and authorization before any actual communication. This article has not sent a message, scheduled a call, inspected a recording or evaluated a sales outcome.

Review the notes without inventing a performance target

A count of questions or a talk-time percentage can describe a recording under a specified measurement method. Neither number alone establishes that the conversation was useful, that the opportunity is qualified or that revenue will follow. This article recommends no universal question count, duration, speaking ratio, response deadline or conversion target.

For preparation, ask whether the note distinguishes known facts from estimates, identifies unanswered questions and accurately labels a proposed next step. Frequently Asked Questions

How many discovery questions should I ask?

This article supplies no universal target. Select questions for the agreed purpose and available time, and leave room for clarification or a change in agenda.

Does a talk-to-listen ratio establish call quality?

No. A percentage alone does not show whether statements were understood, questions were answered or a next step was accepted.

What if the participant cannot quantify a problem?

Record what is known and what is missing. Ask which source could help rather than substituting an invented cost or benefit.

When is a proposed next step confirmed?

Keep a suggestion separate from an arrangement accepted by the responsible participants. An unanswered proposal remains unconfirmed.
